Methodology

How TacticalDirector works

Every step below is real: the same code that renders this page runs the scoring engine and, if you enable it, the AI layer. Nothing here is a marketing simplification of something different happening under the hood.

Describe the encounter

You provide one round of combat: your character (an archetype of warrior, mage, skirmisher, or support, plus current and max HP and a limited resource pool), the enemies present (name and a 1–5 threat tier each), and terrain flags (high ground, cover, hazard). That's the entire input; there's no hidden state carried between rounds.

Four actions, scored against four categories

Attack, Use Ability, Defend, and Retreat are each scored 0–4 across Offensive Value, Survival Risk, Resource Efficiency, and Positional Advantage. For example, Retreat scores a 4 on Survival Risk only once HP drops below 35%; Use Ability's Resource Efficiency depends on how much of the resource pool remains versus how threatening the encounter actually is, so spending your last resource on a weak encounter scores a 0, while spending it on a genuinely dangerous one scores a 4. Every rule is fixed and documented; nothing is learned or guessed.

Averaged and ranked, highest first

An action's overall score is the plain average of its four category scores, mapped onto a tier from Poor to Optimal. All four actions are always shown, ranked highest to lowest; the tool never hides an option or forces a single pick. You can recompute every number by hand from the category scores and reasons on the report.

AI narrates. It never re-scores.

When AI narration is enabled, Claude receives the already-ranked actions and their category scores as structured data and is explicitly instructed to narrate the top recommendation and add a line of "table talk" flavor, not to recompute or reorder anything, and not to invent enemies or rules that weren't given to it. If you disagree with a ranking, the fix is in the scoring rules above, not in the AI layer. The deterministic core works, and is fully tested, with zero dependency on any AI service.

Want the full math, including every scoring rule and tier boundary? It's documented in the repo: scoring methodology and architecture.
